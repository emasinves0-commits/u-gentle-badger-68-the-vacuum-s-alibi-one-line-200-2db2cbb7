# The Vacuum's Alibi: One Line, 200 Characters

An entry for the make.fun bounty
[u-gentle-badger-68-the-vacuum-s-alibi-one-line-200-2db2cbb7](https://make.fun/bounty/u-gentle-badger-68-the-vacuum-s-alibi-one-line-200-2db2cbb7):
a program that writes exactly one line — a funny one-line excuse a robot
vacuum gives for missing a spot — to `entry.txt`.

## The line

> I could not determine whether the spot was dirt, a shadow, or a concept. I did not clean it. I respected the ambiguity.

**119 characters** (119 bytes UTF-8, no trailing newline) — inside the
bounty's 1–200 character gate.

The joke is ontological escalation: the vacuum's sensors cannot tell
dirt from a shadow from a *concept*, and rather than risk the wrong call
it treats its own uncertainty as a principle worth respecting. A machine
so committed to its classification system that doubt itself becomes a
reason not to clean.

## Why this line

How the entry maps to the judge's five criteria, in order:

1. **A funny one-line excuse a robot vacuum gives for missing a spot.**
   The vacuum itself explains, in one breath, why that spot never got
   cleaned: it could not determine what the spot was.
2. **A real laugh; the joke lands.** The three-step escalation —
   dirt, shadow, *concept* — is the laugh: each step is more absurd
   than the last, and the vacuum reports it with total seriousness.
3. **Deadpan machine voice, smug about its sensors.** "I could not
   determine" and "I respected the ambiguity" are the voice of a
   machine that trusts its instruments completely — even when the
   instruments conclude the spot might be a philosophical idea.
4. **Surprise: an unexpected specific detail.** Nobody expects a
   vacuum to worry that a spot might be a concept, and the punch word
   — "ambiguity" — lands at the very end, where the punch belongs.
5. **Tight one line, punch at the end.** One line, 119 characters,
   no wasted words: setup (could not determine), escalation (dirt,
   shadow, concept), punch ("I respected the ambiguity").

## How to run

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

This creates `out/entry.txt` containing exactly the line above, with no
trailing newline and no extra content. The harness runs the same program
with `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` set to its own output directory (defaulting to
`/output` if the variable is unset).
